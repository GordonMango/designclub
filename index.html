About Design Club

デザインクラブについて

Founded in 2026, Design Club is a year-round club where students receive the opportunity to innovate almost anything.

The goal of this club is to equip students from grades 5–12 with the skills and connections to succeed in the field of STEM — a field where there is a real need for adaptability and experience.

These initiatives drive our mission of lifelong learning and global academic excellence.

Our goal

STEM has historically not offered equal access, representation, or opportunity to everyone. Many groups have been underrepresented in engineering, technology, and related fields because of barriers to participation, resources, and mentorship.

Our goal is to help make engineering more accessible and inclusive within our school community. We want every student — regardless of background, experience, or prior exposure to STEM — to have the opportunity to learn, build, compete, and explore engineering.

Through hands-on workshops, collaborative projects, competitions, mentorship, and access to technical resources, we aim to create an environment where students can develop their skills, discover new interests, and feel that they belong in STEM.

STEM competitions are rare

Most STEM competitions require lots of funding and a large student body — the Conrad Challenge, for example, requires more than 10 members. As a student collective, we have a huge advantage: funding from collaborative fundraising and the necessary resources in order to compete.

Competitions & conferences

大会とカンファレンス

What

STEM competitions offer students the opportunity to showcase their innovative products. It is an important part of the design process — being able to receive feedback, and learn from industry-level professionals.

How

In beautiful British Columbia, there are many opportunities that do not involve an extravagant cost. We will begin with a Youth Showcase from the official science fair organization and recycling initiatives, in order to receive merit and fundraise for future competitions that might involve national merit. Competitions are hard to win, so students in this program will receive expert feedback from our principal investigators and possibly industry-level professionals from webinars.

Fundraising & community service

As much harm as engineering and innovation has ramified our environment, technology can also be used as a cornerstone for environmental conservation efforts.

We have decided to join the Electronic Recycling Association’s competition, with a prize pool of $20,000, in order to support members of the club with opportunities to compete in future STEM competitions — many of which, such as FIRST Robotics, require thousands.

All students will have to do is provide their broken hardware to support this national recycling organization. All funding earned from ERA will be provided as bursaries for members of the club to afford parts for their projects, and to enter upcoming competitions.

/ 04

Principal investigators

主任研究員

What is novelty

The best innovations are unique and original — which is what makes them novel.

Why make novelty

Novelty is what makes innovations useful. Imagine a million copies of a single innovation — it doesn’t change anything. A part of this club is to make something useful, but more importantly to make a change in the world. Consequently, we have implemented a PI system where our mentors and experts will support students in ensuring that their projects encompass that unique spark.

Who are the experts

We will do our best to contact experts in many disciplines, such as artificial intelligence, to join our hosted webinars. It is up to students to attend meetings and ask questions that might benefit their project.

At the same time, we acknowledge that this involves a relative amount of trust between students. That is why we will hold 1:1 meetings with students during appropriate times, and ensure that projects between students do not overlap as much as possible.

Design Club is building a student-led team dedicated to creating accessible opportunities in engineering, research, innovation, and competition. Applications for leadership, mentor, and investigator positions will be opening soon.

Seniors

Seniors

/ Strategic plan

Three pillars to success

三つの柱
01

Design and redefine

Members begin by developing original ideas and transforming them into practical designs, prototypes, or engineering solutions. Through feedback from peers, mentors, and industry professionals, students strengthen their technical skills, improve their work, and learn how to approach challenges through an iterative design process.

02

Compete and achieve

Members apply their knowledge and skills through engineering, science, and design competitions. Competition provides students with opportunities to work toward clear objectives, solve problems under real-world constraints, collaborate effectively, and demonstrate their abilities beyond the classroom.

03

Lead and contribute

Members are encouraged to take leadership roles within the club and the broader STEM community. Through mentoring, project management, outreach, and team leadership, students develop the ability to support others, organize meaningful initiatives, and create opportunities for future members to succeed.

Partnerships — As a club starting off, we understand that starting an organization alone is difficult. We do not have the funding necessary, the newest technology, or access to many competitions. Hence, we have decided to collaborate with organizations that support diverse student bodies and mentors with experienced backgrounds to teach us the skills necessary. Thank you to the organizations that have sponsored and supported us.

/ 06

Getting started

Anyone can join. We accept any student in grades 5–12 as long as you are able to participate in events — no prior STEM experience required. Talk to Gordon to get your name on the list.
